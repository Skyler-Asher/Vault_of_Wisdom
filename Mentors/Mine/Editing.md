# The Playbook: Short-Form Editor for Online Fitness Coaches

**Niche sentence:** "I edit short-form videos for online fitness coaches who sell coaching programs."

This is locked for the first 300 outreaches. No switching, no new ideas.

---

## Part 1: The beginner traps (read weekly)

1. **Tool hopping.** Pick one editor and stay on it for 60 days. Don't touch Premiere or DaVinci until you have paying clients.
2. **Tutorial addiction.** Watching is not editing. Rule: for every 1 hour of learning, 2 hours of editing.
3. **Perfectionism before outreach.** Your portfolio only needs to be good enough, not great. Start outreach on day 8, not "when I'm ready."
4. **Blasting before you're ready.** Don't send 100 a day on day 1. Ramp up (schedule below).
5. **Only one DM account.** Instagram limits and bans cold DMs. Expect it to happen.
6. **Free work forever.** Max 3 free samples per week, and none after your first 2 testimonials.
7. **Ignoring the data.** If you don't log it, you're guessing. Every message goes in the tracker.
8. **Quitting at 0 replies.** The first 100-150 messages often get nothing. Judge only after 300.
9. **Undercharging to be liked.** Cheap is fine at the start, free after proof is not.
10. **Working without payment terms.** Always payment upfront, or at least half upfront.

---

## Part 2: Days 1-14, skill training

**Setup (Day 1):** Install your editor. Create a swipe file: save 20 short videos from fitness coaches with 20k-200k followers who post consistently. Note their hook, caption style, pacing, and music.

**Practice footage:** Record yourself talking for 60-90 seconds on a fitness topic (read from a script, it doesn't matter if it's good). Use free stock gym footage from Pexels and Pixabay for b-roll. Don't download other creators' videos for your portfolio.

|Days|Focus|Output|
|---|---|---|
|1-2|Basics: cuts, removing dead air and pauses, auto-captions|2 clips, 30 sec each|
|3-4|Caption styling: bold, high-contrast, highlighted keywords, positioned correctly|2 clips|
|5-6|Hooks: strong first 2 seconds, text hook on screen, zoom punches|2 clips|
|7|Review: compare all clips side by side with your swipe file. Fix the weakest.|Pick best 3|
|8-10|B-roll and sound design: cutaways, sound effects, background music at the right volume|3 new clips|
|11-12|Speed: re-edit the same raw clip in 45 minutes. Speed is your profit margin.|2 clips|
|13-14|Final portfolio: 5-6 best clips in one folder or reel, in vertical 9:16|Portfolio done|

**Quality check before anything goes in your portfolio:**

- Does the first 2 seconds make you want to keep watching?
- Any dead air or awkward pauses left?
- Are captions readable on a phone, with no typos?
- Does it look like it belongs next to your swipe file? If not, redo it.

## Part 3: Daily schedule (from Day 8)

|Time|Task|
|---|---|
|1 hr|Find and qualify prospects (build the day's list)|
|2-3 hrs|Send outreach and replies|
|2 hrs|Custom sample edits (1-3 per day)|
|2 hrs|Skill practice: re-edit, study swipe file, speed drills|
|30 min|Log numbers, review what worked|

**Outreach ramp (this protects your accounts):**

- Week 2 (Days 8-14): 20-30 per day
- Week 3: 50 per day
- Week 4+: scale toward 100 per day, split across email and DMs, and across 2-3 accounts if you need to

New or fresh accounts get restricted fast. Spend 10 minutes a day on the account behaving normally (liking, commenting, posting) so it doesn't look like a bot. Email is more sustainable for 100/day volume: many coaches list an email in their bio.

## Part 4: Finding and qualifying prospects

**Where to look:** Instagram hashtags and search (#onlinefitnesscoach, #fitnesscoach, #onlinecoaching, #transformationcoach), the Following lists of mid-size coaches, and coaching directories.

**Qualify before messaging (30 seconds each):**

- 5k-100k followers
- Posts reels at least 3x per week
- Has a paid offer in the bio (coaching link, application form, "DM to apply")
- Their video quality is average, with plain cuts and weak or no captions, so you can obviously improve it
- Skip anyone with a visible editor, a polished brand, or 500k+ followers

## Part 5: Scripts

**Tier 1: Cold DM (no edit attached):**

> Hey [name], saw your reel on [specific topic]. Your content is solid, but the pacing and captions are leaving views on the table. I edit short-form for online fitness coaches. Want me to send you a free edited version of one of your clips?

Keep it under 3 sentences. One question at the end, so replying is easy.

**Cold email subject lines:** "Quick idea for your [topic] reel" or "Edited one of your clips"

**If they say yes:** Edit a 15-30 second clip from their public reel. Send it with:

> Here's the clip. Took your [topic] reel and tightened the pacing, added captions and a stronger hook. If you like it, I do [8] edits a month for $[X] with 48-hour turnaround. Want to start with your next video?

**Follow-up (3-4 days after no reply):**

> Hey [name], just bumping this. Happy to send that free sample whenever you're ready.

One follow-up only. Then move on.

**If they say "how much?" early:** Don't dodge. Give the price right away.

---

## Part 6: Offer and pricing

- **Starter offer:** 8 short-form edits per month, 48-hour turnaround, 1 round of revisions. $120-200 per month.
- **After 2-3 testimonials:** raise to $250-400.
- Payment upfront for the month. No exceptions.
- After 5 clients, stop taking $100 clients and raise the floor.

## Part 7: Tracking sheet

One row per prospect. Columns: Date, Handle, Followers, Platform, Message type, Replied (Y/N), Sample sent, Interested, Closed, Price, Notes.

**Weekly metrics to calculate:** messages sent, reply rate, sample-to-close rate.

(Build this as a Notion database. See Worries, point 5.)

## Part 8: Checkpoints (decide with numbers)

|After|If this happens|Do this|
|---|---|---|
|100 messages|0 replies|Rewrite the message, check your account is not shadow-limited|
|300 messages|Under 1% replies|Fix message and samples first. Switch niche only if both are fixed and it's still dead.|
|300 messages|Replies but no closes|Your samples or your offer is the problem. Compare yours against the swipe file.|
|First client|Closed|Deliver fast, ask for a testimonial after 2 weeks|
|5 clients|Closed|Raise prices, build a simple process, consider outsourcing|

**Realistic expectation:** weeks 1-2 you earn $0 and you're learning. Weeks 3-6 you might land your first 1-3 clients. Most people who quit do it right before the data starts working.

# Worries

_What I like (locked in):_

- The niche sentence: "I edit short-form videos for online fitness coaches who sell coaching programs."
- The 300-outreach target.

_Most of these are tool friction or comfort issues, not real blockers._

## 1-3. CapCut vs Kdenlive vs Subtitle Edit

The tool doesn't matter. Clients only see the output. CapCut was picked for one reason, easy auto-captions, so if that's gone, the reason is gone too. (CapCut's free tier changes often, so trust what you see.)

Your workflow:

- **Subtitle Edit** (Whisper-based transcription) generates and corrects the captions. It's free and accurate.
- **Kdenlive** does the cuts, zooms, b-roll, and sound.
- **Caption styling** is the weak point in Kdenlive. If you can't match the look of your swipe file there, import your Subtitle Edit file into CapCut just for the styling step, or use the mobile version, which is usually less laggy.

The rule is one main editor for 60 days, and it's Kdenlive. The test: can you make captions that look as good as your swipe file? If yes, done. If not, use the hybrid. Don't switch editors mid-learning.

## 4. Free work forever

Skill doesn't come from free client work. It comes from the 14 days of practice edits, re-editing the same clip faster, and copying your swipe file. Free samples are an outreach cost, capped at 3 per week, and they stop after your first 2 testimonials. Free work is not how you learn.

## 5. Spreadsheets

Skip them. Use a **Notion database** with the same columns (Date, Handle, Followers, Message type, Replied, Sample sent, Closed, Price, Notes). Use a Select or Checkbox for Replied/Closed, and a filter for "Replied = yes". It does the same job. Obsidian works too, but Notion's table view is easier for counting reply rates.

## 6. Payment terms

You don't need a contract. Before your first client, write 4 lines and paste them into every offer:

- Payment upfront for the month, by PayPal, Wise, or Stripe invoice.
- 8 edits per month, 1 round of revisions each.
- Delivery within 48 hours of receiving raw footage.
- No payment means no work starts.

Upfront payment removes almost all of the risk.

## 7. Recording yourself

You don't need to be in front of the camera, and nothing about it has to be public.

- **Private practice raw footage:** record yourself talking and never post it. You edit it to learn, and delete it afterward if you want.
- **Voiceover:** record only your voice over gym stock footage. Your face never appears.
- **Free-licensed footage:** use Creative Commons talks or interviews, but check each license first.

Your portfolio doesn't need you in it. It needs edits that look like what coaches post.

Practicing on your own face also helps your conversation skills. Cutting out your "ums" and pauses shows you how you actually talk. In chat, you can also write answers to practice questions and I'll critique them, since client calls will need that skill eventually.

## 8. B-roll

B-roll is any footage that isn't the main talking shot. In a coach's video, the main shot (A-roll) is them talking to the camera, and the b-roll is cutaways: someone squatting while they explain squats, a food prep clip, a transformation photo. It keeps the video visually moving and adds meaning. Pexels and Pixabay have free gym footage you can use.
